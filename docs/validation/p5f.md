No P5F multiplayer result is claimed. The required trial needs at least two real people to join the same real Dota instance through the player flow, observe normal lifecycle and full reclaim, and record natural request/assignment/create/Ready/JoinInfo timestamps. The owner has now confirmed one real person entering a maintenance instance and normal gameplay; this was outside the player flow and does not substitute for the two-person P5F trial. Bots, browser sessions and unit tests do not substitute for this trial.

Owner-provided P4 carry-over hypothesis: the template's Steam login success Ready marker can vary after other startup conditions are already satisfied, and may explain earlier roughly 55-second `create_started_at → ready_at` samples. P5F should correlate existing create, process/startup, other Ready markers, Steam login success, d2core Ready and Platform `ready_at` evidence where logs permit. Preserve the Steam login condition and JoinInfo gate; do not infer scheduler regression from that wait. Controller's roughly five-second claim polling is a separate future optimization candidate. No d2core protocol or large telemetry/performance change is authorized by this observation alone.

During P5A/P5B template validation, a drained Linux `dota` mode instance offered a concrete diagnostic example: an intermediate official d2core status showed the process running and the loading/host-activate/manual-setup markers matched, while the Steam login success marker was absent and the room remained `loading`. A later status showed the Steam login marker and `ready`; the instance was then fully reclaimed. This was a direct maintenance instance, not a Platform Allocation, so it provides no Platform scheduler latency sample or player time-to-join measurement. Correlate its preserved d2core operation/engine logs with future P5F Platform samples rather than treating the intermediate state as an error.

After the owner's one-person maintenance test, a separate normal player API request for the same content recorded `requested_at` 12:20:20.066 UTC, `assigned_at` 12:20:21.925, `create_started_at` 12:20:24.432, `ready_at` and `join_info_available_at` 12:20:44.371, and `reclaimed_at` 12:20:54.378. Thus request-to-JoinInfo was 24.3 seconds, with create-started-to-Ready 19.9 seconds. This request reached valid JoinInfo and full reclaim but had no human player join; keep it separate from multiplayer evidence. Existing d2core logs may be correlated later without changing Ready or telemetry.

The owner also entered separate `dota` and `hard` maintenance instances and confirmed normal gameplay in each. Subsequent normal player API probes, again without a human join, recorded request-to-JoinInfo of 35.0 and 45.5 seconds and create-started-to-Ready of 29.9 and 39.9 seconds, respectively. An old-game `n6` probe took 30.9 seconds request-to-JoinInfo and 24.9 seconds create-started-to-Ready. All three reached valid JoinInfo and full reclaim. These few samples are evidence for later log correlation, not a performance distribution or proof that Steam login caused any particular interval.
